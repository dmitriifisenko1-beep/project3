Vladivostok

Primorsky Krai, Russia

Welcome to My Hometown

Vladivostok is my hometown, a port city on the Sea of Japan in the far southeast of Russia. It wraps around Golden Horn Bay at the southern tip of the Muravyov-Amursky Peninsula and is the final stop of the Trans-Siberian Railway. Visitors come to see its giant bridges, its large modern aquarium, the old forts that once guarded the harbor, and a history shaped by its neighbors: in the early 1900s the city had large Chinese, Korean, and Japanese communities. It is my favorite place to visit because it is where I grew up, and because so much of the city's story can be seen along its coastline.

Top Attractions

Russky Bridge

Opened in July 2012, the Russky Bridge crosses the Eastern Bosphorus Strait to connect mainland Vladivostok with Russky Island. Its main span is 1,104 meters (3,622 feet) long, making it the second-longest cable-stayed bridge in the world.

Primorsky Aquarium

On Russky Island, the Primorsky Aquarium is Russia's largest public aquarium, with nearly 500 species and close to 10,000 animals. The building is designed to look like a giant clamshell, and its main tank includes a 70-meter underwater tunnel. The aquarium is open Tuesday through Sunday and closed on Mondays.

Vladivostok Fortress

Construction of the Vladivostok Fortress began in 1877, and the city was officially declared a fortress in 1889. Its forts and batteries spread across about 400 square kilometers, from the mainland peninsula to Russky and Popov Islands, and the fortress never had to fight a battle. Primorye's official tourism site calls it the most powerful sea fortification complex in Russia. The Fortress Museum, in an old artillery battery overlooking Sportivnaya Harbor, displays cannons outside and photos and weapons inside.

Arseniev Museum of Far East History

Established in 1884, this was the first local history museum in the Russian Far East, and today it is the largest museum in Primorsky Krai. It is named after Vladimir Arseniev, an explorer of the Far East. Its collections cover the region's natural history, indigenous cultures, and archaeology, including Ice Age fossils. The main building is at 20 Svetlanskaya Street, which began as Amerikanskaya Street, the city's very first street.

About the City

  • Population: 603,519 people (2021 census), making it the largest city in Primorsky Krai and its administrative center.
  • Founded: July 2, 1860, as a Russian military outpost on Golden Horn Bay.
  • Geography: located around Golden Horn Bay on the Sea of Japan, at the southern end of the Muravyov-Amursky Peninsula.
  • Weather: cold, dry winters with many clear, sunny days, and warm, humid, rainy summers. Typhoons and tropical storms sometimes reach the city.
  • Language: Russian.
  • Time zone: UTC+10, seven hours ahead of Moscow.
  • Fun fact: the Trans-Siberian Railway from Moscow to Vladivostok is 9,289 kilometers long, the longest railway line in the world.
